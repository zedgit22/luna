Lunar Regolith Plant Growth Simulation: Scientific and Mathematical Background

Waste-to-nutrient conversion, Fe-catalyzed nitrile cracking, and resource-limited logistic growth

Overview

Nitrile-rich Waste (e.g., Gloves) provides a nitrogen source. Iron (Fe) in Lunar Regolith acts as a catalyst for nitrile cracking to nitrogen (N). Plant biomass grows with limitations from N, H2O, and O2, and is bounded by an environmental carrying capacity K.

Figure 1. Flow: Nitrile from Gloves is cracked to N via Fe; growth limited by N/H2O/O2 and bounded by K.
  • Fe in regolith acts as a catalyst for nitrile-to-N conversion.
  • Biomass B(t) grows subject to resource limits (N, H2O, O2) and a logistic bound K.
  • We use saturation functions and Liebig’s minimum (min) to combine limits.

Mathematical Model Simplified

1) Nitrile-to-Nitrogen Conversion (Fe-catalyzed)

N_production_rate = k_base · M_nitrile
                   + k_fe · Fe% · η_catalyst · M_nitrile
M_nitrile(t+Δt) = max(0, M_nitrile(t) − N_production_rate · Δt)
N_ppm(t+Δt)     = N_ppm(t) + α · N_production_rate · Δt

2) Resource Limitations (N, H2O, O2)

f_N  = N / (N_sat + N)
f_W  = W / (W_sat + W)
f_O2 = O2 / (O2_sat + O2)
resource_limit = min(f_N, f_W, f_O2)
Figure 2. Saturation (Michaelis–Menten-like) limiting functions for N, H2O, and O2.

3) Logistic Growth with Carrying Capacity

dB/dt = r_max · resource_limit · B · (1 − B/K)

As B → K, (1 − B/K) → 0, so growth slows. K is an environmental property (space, light, substrate). In a derived-K variant, K is computed from resource budgets.
